# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Argue about the work, not about the person doing it.

## What that means here

This project is maintained in public by a small team, and most of what happens
in it is review — someone reading someone else's work and saying what is wrong
with it. That is the interaction most likely to go badly, so it is the one worth
being explicit about:

- **Critique the change, not the author.** "This drops the site scope" is
  review. "You clearly did not read the code" is not, and it makes the first
  sentence harder to hear.
- **Assume the other person had a reason.** Ask what it was before explaining
  why it is wrong. It is frequently a reason.
- **Take no for an answer.** A change can be well made and still not belong in
  this repository. That decision is the maintainers', it will come with a
  reason, and re-opening the argument in three places is not an appeal.
- **Nobody owes you a review on your schedule.** This is not a support contract.
  A ping after a week is fine; a ping a day is not.

Unacceptable, and acted on rather than debated: harassment, personal or
demographic insults, sexual attention or imagery, publishing anybody's private
information, and sustained disruption of a discussion. Politics, nationality,
religion and identity are off-topic in this repository — not because they do not
matter, but because they are not what anyone came here to resolve.

## Scope

Issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, and anywhere someone is
representing this project.

## Reporting

Email **hey@getopen.so**. Reports are read by the maintainers and kept
confidential; we will not act on one without talking to you first, unless
somebody is in danger.

There is no committee and no appeals process, because there are two of us and
inventing one would be theatre. What there is: we will tell you what we decided
and why. Consequences run from "please stop" through an edit or a lock, to a
block from the organization.

If your report is about a maintainer, say so and send it anyway — one of us is
not the other.

## Why this is not the Contributor Covenant

The Covenant is the default and is a perfectly good document. It is also long
enough that nobody reads it, which makes it furniture rather than a rule. This
says the same things in the words we would use if we had to enforce them.
